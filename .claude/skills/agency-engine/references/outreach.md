# Outreach

## The rule that governs every message

The first line must prove you looked at *them*. Not "I came across your company"
— that proves nothing and every recipient has learned to delete on it. Name the
req and how long it's been open, name the ad they're running, name the round they
raised. Specificity is the only thing separating you from the fifty other
messages in that inbox this morning.

Second rule: one ask, and make it small. "Worth a quick look?" outperforms
"do you have 30 minutes Tuesday?" by a wide margin, because the second one asks
them to open a calendar and the first asks them to type one word.

Third rule: never attach anything to a cold email. Attachments trip spam filters
and signal mass send. Links are fine, one of them, below the fold.

## Sequence — stale requisition (Channel 1)

Five touches over 14 days, then stop. A sixth touch converts nobody and gets you
marked as spam.

**Day 1 — Email**

> Subject: your Senior Motion Designer role
>
> Hi {First} — saw the Senior Motion Designer role has been open since July.
> Guessing that's not for lack of trying.
>
> We run a vetted bench of motion designers and place them on monthly retainers —
> senior person producing inside a week, no hiring risk, cancel any month. Most
> clients use it either as a bridge while they keep searching, or find they stop
> searching.
>
> Worth a quick look?
>
> {Name}

Why it works: it names a specific, verifiable fact; it acknowledges their effort
rather than implying incompetence; "bridge while you keep searching" removes the
threat to a decision they've already publicly committed to; and it asks for
almost nothing.

**Day 3 — LinkedIn connection**, no pitch in the note. Just: *"Saw you're hiring
for motion — sent you a note on email, no worries if it's not a fit."* You are
buying profile-view recall, not a reply.

**Day 5 — Email, reply in thread**

> Quick add — the reason I flagged it: a $150k req is about $15.5k/month fully
> loaded, plus the recruiter fee when it lands. Our retainers for the same output
> run $8–10k and start Monday.
>
> Happy to send two or three examples of comparable work if useful.

**Day 9 — Email, new thread, new angle.** Drop the role entirely and lead with a
proof point: a similar company, what shipped, in what timeframe. Same close.

**Day 14 — Break-up**

> Going to stop here so I'm not cluttering your inbox. If the role fills, genuinely
> glad. If it's still open in a few months and you want a stopgap, we're easy to
> find.

The break-up reliably outperforms every message before it. It costs the recipient
nothing to reply and removes the pressure that made them ignore you.

## Sequence — DTC brand (Channel 2)

Three touches, and the first one has to contain a real observation or don't send it.

**Day 1**

> Subject: your {product} ads
>
> Hi {First} — went through what {Brand} is running right now. The {specific
> concept} is doing the heavy lifting and it looks like it's been live a while;
> the other three are variations on it.
>
> That's usually where CPA starts drifting — not a targeting problem, a volume
> problem. We produce 20+ new concepts a month against a brand's proven angles so
> there's always something fresh entering rotation.
>
> Want me to send three concepts for {product}, free, so you can see the standard?

**Day 4** — actually send the three concepts if they didn't reply. This is the
highest-converting move in the entire playbook and almost nobody does it, because
it costs real work up front. Do it for prospects worth $8k/month or more.

**Day 10** — break-up, same shape as above.

## Sequence — agency overflow (Channel 3)

One message. Short. These people are busy and either need a bench or don't.

> Subject: white-label bench — {craft}
>
> Hi {First} — we run a vetted freelance bench for {motion / design / editing} and
> work white-label under agency brands. Typical turnaround {X days}, we never
> contact your client, everything goes out under your name.
>
> If you ever get caught short on capacity, worth having us on file. Happy to send
> rates and a couple of samples.

Then follow up once, 10 days later, and leave it. Overflow relationships convert
on *their* timeline — the moment they oversell — not yours. The goal is to be in
the file when that happens.

## Objection handling

Answer the objection, then re-ask for the small thing. Never argue.

**"We'd rather hire someone full-time."**
Good — most clients should. Retainers work well as a bridge: senior output while
you keep searching, and you can cancel the month you sign someone. Several of our
clients started that way. Want to see what that looks like for the role?

**"Too expensive."**
Compared to the salary or compared to a freelancer? Against the $150k req it's
roughly half fully loaded. Against a freelancer, you're right that we're more —
you're paying for someone else having vetted them, managing them, and being
accountable when it's wrong. If budget is genuinely the constraint, tell me the
number and I'll tell you honestly what fits it.

**"We already have an agency."**
Makes sense. Most brands our size use us alongside one — the agency does strategy
and campaigns, we do volume. Different job. If the volume side is ever the
bottleneck, worth a conversation.

**"Send me some info."**
Usually a soft no, occasionally real. Send one page, not a deck, and put a
specific next step in the same message: *"Sent. If it looks close, I'll hold
Thursday 2pm — say the word."* A "send info" with no proposed next step dies.

**"Who actually does the work?"**
The honest answer is your best answer, and rehearse it: a vetted senior
specialist from our network, matched to your brief. We manage them, QA everything
before it reaches you, and you deal with us. You'll know who they are, and you'll
work with the same person throughout. Never obscure this — clients respect the
network model, and being cagey about it is what makes it sound like something to
hide.

## What not to do

- No fake urgency, no invented scarcity ("only taking two clients this quarter").
  Buyers at this level have seen it and it prices you as small.
- No sending the same message to five people at one company. They talk.
- No claiming clients or work you don't have. It surfaces in one reference call
  and it's unrecoverable.
- No posting a role you won't hire from to harvest portfolios. Beyond being
  deceptive, it poisons the only asset that compounds here — freelancers talk to
  each other far more than clients do, and the roster is the business.
