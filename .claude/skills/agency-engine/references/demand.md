# Demand: where clients come from

Demand is the constraint. Everything else in the business is solvable in a day;
this is the part that isn't. Work the channels in this order — they're ranked by
speed to first dollar, not by size of eventual prize.

## Channel 1 — Stale job requisitions (start here)

A company with a creative role open 45+ days has already done three things for
you: admitted the need, gotten budget approved, and failed to solve it. That's a
qualified lead that raised its own hand in public.

The budget math you're arguing against is explicit: a $150k posted salary is
roughly $12.5k/month, and fully loaded (payroll tax, benefits, equipment,
software) it's closer to $15.5k. Add the recruiter fee they'll pay if they ever
fill it — typically 20% of first-year salary, $30k — and every additional month
of vacancy is work not getting done. A $9k/month retainer starting Monday is
cheaper, faster, and cancellable. That's the entire pitch.

**How to find them**

Search LinkedIn Jobs, Indeed, and company career pages for:

- Titles: Senior Content Designer, Brand Designer, Motion Designer, Video Editor,
  Creative Director, Content Marketing Manager, Social Media Manager, Email
  Marketing Manager, Growth Designer
- Posted: 45+ days ago (this is the whole filter — recency is the signal inverted)
- Company size: 50–500. Below 50 there's rarely budget for a retainer; above 500
  procurement will eat six weeks.
- Reposted listings are the strongest signal of all: it means the first attempt
  failed and someone is now personally embarrassed about it.

**Who to contact**: not HR or the recruiter — they are measured on filling the
req, and you are proposing they don't. Go to the hiring manager, one level above
the role: Head of Brand, VP Marketing, Creative Director. They're the one whose
work isn't getting done.

Sequence and templates: `outreach.md`.

## Channel 2 — Ecommerce and DTC brands

Brands doing $1–10M/year burn creative faster than any other buyer: 20–50 ad
variations a month, email flows, landing pages, product photography. They cannot
hire for that volume and they know it. Retainers land at $5–15k/month.

**Buying signals, strongest first**

- Running ads *right now* with visibly tired creative — the same three concepts
  recycled for months. That brand's performance is decaying and they know it.
- Recently launched or repositioned — new product line, rebrand, new market.
- Hiring for a marketing role (this is Channel 1 in disguise; treat it as such).
- Active on a stack that implies scale: Klaviyo, Shopify Plus, a real ad spend.

Use whatever ad-library and store research tooling is available in the session to
check what a prospect is actually running before you contact them. Naming a
specific ad of theirs in the first line is the difference between a 2% and a 15%
reply rate.

**The angle**: not "we make nice creative." It's *creative volume as a
performance lever* — "you're running four concepts, your best performer is 60
days old, and fatigue is why CPA is climbing. We ship 20 new concepts a month
against your top angles." Sell the diagnosis, not the deliverable.

## Channel 3 — Agency overflow (white label)

Established agencies routinely win more than they can staff. You become their
bench. You take 30–40% less per hour than a direct client would pay, but you
spend zero on sales and the work recurs without a pitch.

This is the least glamorous and most reliable channel in the business. Two or
three agency relationships put a floor under revenue while you build direct
clients on top. Most brokerages should live here in year one and never fully
leave.

**How to open one**: contact the operations lead or head of production, not the
founder. The message is short and unsexy: what crafts you cover, typical
turnaround, that you white-label under their brand and never contact their
client. That last promise is the entire product — say it explicitly.

**Watch the margin.** Agency overflow work naturally sits on rung 1 of the margin
ladder. Accept it as a floor, not as the business.

## Channel 4 — Recently funded companies

A seed or Series A raise means budget, no team, and pressure to show traction
fast. Hit them within 60 days of the announcement, before they've hired.

Filter on announcement date and round size ($2M+). The pitch writes itself: they
need to look bigger than they are, immediately, and hiring takes a quarter they
don't have.

Lower volume than the other channels, higher deal size, and they churn when they
finally hire in-house — which is fine if you've priced for it.

## Coverage math

Pipeline coverage is the only leading indicator that matters. Everything else
tells you about the past.

- Cold outbound converts at roughly 1–3% to a booked call.
- Booked calls convert at 20–30% to a signed retainer.
- So one new client needs ~200–400 well-targeted contacts, or ~15 booked calls.
- Carry **3× pipeline coverage** against your monthly new-revenue target: to add
  $10k/month in new retainers, hold $30k of weighted pipeline.

If coverage is below 3×, no other work matters this week. `pipeline.py report`
computes this against live state — check it before deciding what to do with a day.
