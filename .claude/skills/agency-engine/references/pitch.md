# Pitch: discovery, pricing, proposal, close

## Discovery call (25 minutes, and keep it to 25)

You are diagnosing, not presenting. The client should talk for 20 of the 25
minutes. A call where you talked more than they did is a call you lost.

Run it in this order:

1. **Situation** — "Walk me through how creative gets made today." Listen for who
   currently does it and what breaks.
2. **Volume** — "How much are you producing a month? How much do you *want* to be?"
   The gap is the deal size.
3. **Cost of the gap** — "What doesn't happen because of it?" This is the most
   important question on the call. Make them say the consequence out loud —
   launches slipping, ad performance decaying, the founder editing video at
   midnight. You will quote against this number, not against your costs.
4. **Prior attempts** — "What have you tried?" Freelancers that flaked, an agency
   that was too slow, a req that won't fill. Tells you what to differentiate on.
5. **Decision** — "If this made sense, what happens next on your side?" Who signs,
   what budget exists, what timeline. Ask it plainly; nobody is offended by it.

Close the call with a commitment, not a pleasantry: *"I'll have a proposal to you
tomorrow. Can we put 15 minutes on Thursday to walk through it?"* A proposal sent
into silence converts at a fraction of one with a review call attached.

## Pricing

**Price against their alternative, never against your cost.** Their alternative
is a salary, an agency, or the status quo bleeding money. Your contractor cost is
your business and is not an input to the number.

Anchor with the salary math whenever a req exists:

| | Their alternative | Your retainer |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly cost | $15,500 fully loaded | $9,000 |
| Time to output | 2–4 months | 1 week |
| Recruiter fee | ~$30,000 | none |
| Exit cost | severance, awkwardness | 30 days' notice |

**Retainer bands** — pick by output volume, then sanity-check the margin:

| Band | Monthly | Typical scope | Contractor cost | Gross margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | $4–6k | one craft, light volume | $1.6–2.4k | 60% |
| Core | $8–12k | one craft, full-time-equivalent output | $3.2–4.8k | 60% |
| Multi | $15–25k | two or three crafts, managed | $6–10k | 58–60% |
| Project | fixed | defined deliverable, 4–8 weeks | ≤40% of fee | 60%+ |

**The margin floor is 50%.** Run `economics.py engagement` before you send any
number. Below the floor, the fix is scope, not price — cut deliverables until the
margin works, and show the client the smaller scope at the price they can pay.
Discounting past the floor turns you into an employee of your contractor.

**Never quote hourly to a client.** Hourly caps your revenue at your contractor's
speed, punishes you for using a faster person, and turns every invoice into a
timesheet argument. Quote a monthly retainer against a defined output volume
("up to 20 concepts, 5 revisions each"), or a fixed project fee.

**Always quote three options.** One option is a yes/no; three is a *which*. Put
your target in the middle, make the top one genuinely better rather than a straw
man, and let the bottom one be small enough that "no" feels like an overreaction.
A meaningful share of buyers take the top tier, and none of them would have if
you'd offered one price.

**Annual prepay discount: 10%.** It's the cheapest capital you will ever raise —
no interest, no dilution, no covenant — and prepaid contracts read as locked
revenue to any future buyer. Offer it on every Core-band deal and above.

## Proposal

One page, sent within 24 hours. A deck signals slow and expensive; a page signals
you've done this before. Use `assets/proposal-template.md`.

Structure, in this order and no other:

1. **What you told me** — their situation and the cost of the gap, in their words.
   This is the section that closes the deal. If they read nothing else and feel
   understood, you win.
2. **What we'll do** — deliverables and volume, concretely. Numbers, not adjectives.
3. **How it works** — brief, produce, review, ship. Name the cadence and who they
   talk to.
4. **Three options** — with the middle one marked "most common."
5. **Terms** — start date, 30-day notice, invoiced monthly in advance.
6. **Next step** — one sentence, one action.

No credentials section, no team bios, no "about us." They already decided you were
credible enough to take the call.

## Showing work

You will show work your roster did for other clients. That is normal and fine, and
there is exactly one way to do it that doesn't create liability:

- Written permission from the freelancer to show it (the roster agreement in
  `assets/portfolio-release.md` covers this at onboarding — get it then, not when
  you need it).
- Attributed honestly: **"Produced by {name}, from our network"** or **"Work by
  our roster"**. Never "our work" if it wasn't done for you.
- Client confidentiality respected — if the end client's contract restricts
  publicity, anonymize the brand or don't show it.

The network framing is not a weakness to paper over. "We have 40 vetted specialists
and we'll put the right one on this" beats "we personally did all of this" for any
buyer above a certain size, because it's the answer to *what happens when you're
busy or your one person quits*.

## Closing

- **Review the proposal live.** Screen-share, walk the page, stop talking after
  the options. Whoever speaks first after the price loses; let it be them.
- **Ask directly**: "Does this look like something you want to do?" Not "any
  questions?" — that invites a stall.
- **Handle the stall, don't accept it.** "Let me think about it" gets one
  question: "Totally fair — what's the part you're unsure about?" The real
  objection is behind that answer, and you cannot address it if you let them go.
- **14-day kill rule.** No movement in 14 days, mark it lost in state and move on.
  Put it in a 90-day re-touch list. The cost of a lingering deal isn't the deal,
  it's the attention it steals from the next one.
- **Contract goes out same day as verbal yes.** Enthusiasm decays fast, and the
  gap between "yes" and "signed" is where deals die. Have `assets/client-msa.md`
  and a filled SOW ready to send inside the hour.
